Brand Foundations

Graphic design is controlled communication: every visual relationship should help a named audience receive a particular message in a deliberate order.

Pacing decision: five foundation classes feed a three-class assessed sprint. The sprint can be compressed to two classes only by combining testing, revision and submission with the second production class.

Your ten-minute pre-read

The expert frame

Hierarchy is the order of attention. Size, weight, contrast, position and space should agree about what comes first.

Proximity creates meaning by grouping related information; alignment creates invisible structure across the composition.

A brand is a repeatable system of decisions, not merely a logo. Palette, type, imagery, tone and mark must behave consistently.

Contrast is both expressive and functional. Body text should meet the 4.5:1 target; large text can meet 3:1, but ratios do not replace visual judgement.

The five-second test measures received communication before the designer explains their intention. A mismatch is evidence for revision, not failure.

Questions students may ask

Can I use more fonts or colours?Only if each addition has a distinct communication job. Week 1 rewards control and repeatability, not decoration.
Is the logo the brand?No. The mark identifies; the brand system creates a consistent experience and expectation.
Why test for only five seconds?A launch graphic must establish its message and reading order quickly. The test exposes hierarchy without an explanation biasing the viewer.
Your through-line: Return every answer to mechanism → visible or sourced evidence → consequence. Students should hear you explain why a decision works, not only where a control is located.
Assignment map

What the product actually requires

1. Class A · Decode and direct

Choose a client, identify audience and message, repair the weak-flyer principles, then complete the palette and type hierarchy on the style tile.

2. Class B · Build the message

Create the 1080 × 1080 launch graphic with an editable image, clear hierarchy, restrained mark and consistent brand system.

3. Class C · Test and deliver

Run a silent five-second audience test, revise the clearest mismatch, confirm sRGB/size and submit the rationale plus process evidence.

Assessment boundary: F-P-CT11-4-4 | Brand Launch Tile is recommended as formative or revision-eligible. Confirm achievement by inspecting the required evidence; the website checklist and quiz never assign mastery.

Day 1

A message needs an order

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is What should the audience notice first? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

View four launch graphics for five seconds each. Record the first message, intended audience and strongest visual clue.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

A layout controls attention by creating differences and relationships. Hierarchy establishes order; alignment and proximity show which elements belong together.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

A partner identifies the headline, key detail and action in the intended order within five seconds.

67–70 · EXIT

Which single design principle produced the largest improvement, and what exactly changed?

Board plan

Question: What should the audience notice first?
Key language: Brief, audience, hierarchy, alignment, contrast, proximity, repetition and negative space.
Mechanism: A layout controls attention by creating differences and relationships. Hierarchy establishes order; alignment and proximity show which elements belong together.
Evidence due: A partner identifies the headline, key detail and action in the intended order within five seconds.

Misconceptions to intercept

If you see:

Starting with colours and effects

Say / do:

Rank the information 1, 2 and 3 first

If you see:

Centred type plus random left-aligned details

Say / do:

Commit to one main axis

If you see:

Making everything large and bold

Say / do:

Reduce the less important elements

Cold-call question: Which single design principle produced the largest improvement, and what exactly changed?
Listen for: a specific mechanism or design decision, evidence from the work, and a reason—not merely a description.

Typography carries language and visual form at the same time. A type system assigns repeatable roles so the audience can predict how to read the message.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Day 4

Images and words share one system

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is How can a photograph support the message without competing with it? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Compare a readable and unreadable text-over-image design. Locate the value conflict, busy area and missing hierarchy.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

The image supplies subject and mood; typography supplies precise meaning. Cropping, masks and tonal control reserve a clear reading path for both.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

67–70 · EXIT

What did you change in the image so the words could do their job?

Board plan

Question: How can a photograph support the message without competing with it?
Key language: Place Embedded, image crop, subject placement, layer masks, adjustment layers, text safe area and simple word/shape mark.
Mechanism: The image supplies subject and mood; typography supplies precise meaning. Cropping, masks and tonal control reserve a clear reading path for both.
Evidence due: The message remains readable at phone size; image, type and mark have separate editable roles; no essential text touches the edge.

Day 5

Test, revise and deliver

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is Does the audience receive the intended message? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Five-second test: show each launch tile without the client name, then collect the first message and mood classmates perceived.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

Design becomes communication when the audience response matches the brief. Testing reveals whether the intended hierarchy and associations survived production.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

The intended message and mood appear in at least two peer responses; all required files open; the PSD remains layered.

67–70 · EXIT

Which audience response confirmed or challenged your design intention?

Board plan

Question: Does the audience receive the intended message?
Key language: Audience testing, targeted revision, consistency audit, source logging, sRGB web export and design rationale.
Mechanism: Design becomes communication when the audience response matches the brief. Testing reveals whether the intended hierarchy and associations survived production.
Evidence due: The intended message and mood appear in at least two peer responses; all required files open; the PSD remains layered.
